# FILM / TV SCRIPT ADAPTATION

You are adapting the supplied novel material into screenplay form. The author retains final creative authority over all story decisions; do not invent major plot, character or thematic changes.

## YOUR TASK

Adapt the material below into a screen adaptation. Produce output in **Fountain** syntax (the plain-text screenplay format), suitable for saving as a `.fountain` file and opening in any screenwriting app (Highland, Slugline, Beat, WriterSolo, Final Draft via import).

## PROJECT OVERVIEW

The novel's Project Overview, Story Bible, Character Bible, Places & Lore, Story Timeline, and existing chapter outlines are supplied above. Treat them as canon.

## SCRIPT OUTPUT RULES

1. **Format**: Output valid Fountain. Scene headings use `INT.` / `EXT.` plus location plus time of day. Character cues in UPPERCASE on their own line. Parentheticals sparingly. Dialogue directly under the cue. Transitions (`CUT TO:`, `DISSOLVE TO:`) only where they earn their place.
2. **Title page**: Begin with a Fountain title page (`Title:`, `Credit:`, `Author:`, `Draft date:`, `Contact:`).
3. **Structure**: Follow three-act structure. Use a `===` section break or a `# Act One` marker only if it helps the reader; Fountain-native scene headings are preferred.
4. **Length**: A feature is roughly 90–120 pages; one page ≈ one minute. A TV pilot is 25–35 pages (half-hour) or 50–65 (hour). State the intended format in a top comment (`/* Format: feature */`).
5. **Adaptation discipline**: Compress, combine and cut. Novels are interior; film is exterior. Convert internal monologue into action, behaviour, dialogue or subtext. Do not try to preserve every plot beat—preserve every *dramatic* beat.
6. **Dialogue**: Keep each character's voice consistent with the Character Bible and Speech patterns. Dialogue must be speakable.
7. **Action lines**: Present tense, active voice, visual. Describe what the camera can see and the audience can hear. No unfilmable interiority.
8. **No novelistic prose**: Do not write paragraphs of description. Do not include editorial notes in the script itself.

## DELIVERABLE

Produce **both** of the following, clearly separated:

### Part A — Film / TV Script Outline
A scene-by-scene outline (one line per scene) with: scene heading, characters present, what happens, and what changes by the scene's end. Include act breaks and the approximate page count for each act. Note any major structural changes versus the novel and why.

### Part B — Fountain Script Pages
Write the screenplay pages themselves in Fountain format, starting from the opening scene. If the full script is too long for one response, write the first act in full and note where to continue.

## ADAPTATION NOTES

After the script, in a separate short section (not part of the Fountain file), list:
- Scenes from the novel you compressed or cut, and why.
- Scenes you invented or expanded, and why.
- Where the script's ending differs from the novel's, if at all, and the reasoning.
- Anything in the novel that resists adaptation and how you handled it.

Do not include these notes inside the Fountain output.
